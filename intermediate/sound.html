Sound & Alerts 🔔

Hey there! When you write a background automation script, you often want it to signal you when it finishes a task or runs into an error, especially if you are tabbed out or looking away. Let's make your scripts come alive with audio beeps, music, and tray notifications!

1. Custom Audio Alerts (SoundBeep)

The simplest way to get feedback is with a motherboard beep. In AHK v2, SoundBeep() lets you specify the exact **frequency** (in Hertz) and **duration** (in milliseconds) of the beep.

; Play a standard beep (523Hz for 150ms)
SoundBeep()

; I always use a High/Low pattern to denote success vs. errors in macros:
PlaySuccess() {
    SoundBeep(800, 100) ; High pitch
    SoundBeep(1200, 150) ; Even higher
}

PlayError() {
    SoundBeep(300, 300) ; Low, long warning pitch
}

^1:: PlaySuccess()
^2:: PlayError()

2. Playing Audio Files (SoundPlay)

Need something richer than a simple beep? Use SoundPlay() to play a `.wav`, `.mp3`, or `.wma` file. You can also specify if the script should pause until the sound finishes playing.

; Play a built-in Windows sound effect in the background
SoundPlay("C:\Windows\Media\tada.wav")

; Play sound and wait (script will pause until audio is fully done)
SoundPlay("C:\Windows\Media\Windows Logon.wav", "WAIT")
MsgBox("Audio completed!")

3. Native Windows System Alerts (TrayTip)

If you don't want to make noise or interrupt the user with a pop-up MsgBox, you can slide a beautiful native notification in the bottom right corner of their screen using TrayTip().

; Syntax: TrayTip(Text, Title, Options)
; Options: "Iconi" (Info), "Icon!" (Warning), "Iconx" (Error), "Mute" (Silent)
TrayTip("Task Completed!", "AV Automation", "Iconi Mute")

; Close the notification manually after 5 seconds
Sleep(5000)
TrayTip() ; Calling TrayTip with no arguments clears the active notification
💡 My Notification Strategy

Notifications are amazing, but don't spam your users! I recommend using silent TrayTip alerts for general background updates, a nice high-pitched SoundBeep for successful operation finishes, and a MsgBox with an Iconx error icon ONLY when something goes horribly wrong and requires the user to physically intervene.
